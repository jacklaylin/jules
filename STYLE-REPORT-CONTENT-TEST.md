# Jules style report: first content test

Status: planning draft; no application changes.

All three people and their source records below are fictional test fixtures. They are not customer analyses or verified commerce records. Product prices, stock, sizing, and retailer policies are intentionally absent. These reports test editorial choices, evidence requirements, and voice; real submissions are needed to validate extraction and whether people recognize themselves.

Voice source: [VOICE.md](VOICE.md). The report uses its shopper voice, with the founder-requested tone controls. Humor stays about clothing and shopping choices. Facts do not change with tone.

## Tone check using the same supported facts

Sample B's sweater card is the controlled comparison. Do not change evidence, counts, or saved preferences when the user changes tone.

- Be nice: “You ordered four navy sweaters and kept two. The color was right; finding the length you wanted took a few tries.”
- Balanced: “You ordered four navy sweaters and kept two. You know what you like; getting the length right took a little more work.”
- Roast me: “Four navy sweaters ordered, two kept. Apparently buying a navy sweater needed auditions.”

The sharper version is optional. If the joke sounds forced or doesn't suit the user's feedback, use the direct observation. Do not force a punchline into every card.

## Card-selection requirements for the MVP

1. Use a small library of cards with explicit evidence requirements. Reports may have different lengths.
2. Select for distinctiveness, support, and shopping usefulness. Purchase volume is not a measure of personal style.
3. Keep the starter pack as the common centerpiece when inputs support one. For extremely sparse inputs, ask for another outfit or confirmed staple rather than filling it with guesses.
4. Merge overlapping cards unless the additional card contributes a distinct, useful conclusion.
5. Put numerical calculations in deterministic application logic when implemented; the writing step receives the checked result and its sample coverage.
6. Record source references, confidence, user confirmation, and context for each inference. Explicit corrections override conflicting guesses.
7. Before the reveal, ask at most three consequential confirmation questions. Save unconfirmed inferences as hypotheses, not confirmed preferences.
8. Keep facts and share selections stable when switching tone. Changes to facts must regenerate affected cards and invalidate stale exports.
9. Exports show only the selected card content and approved imagery. No evidence panel, source IDs, receipt details, or private credentials. Spending, sizes, and return information require explicit selection to share.
10. Add the Jules watermark and the confirmed public signup address during implementation. Do not ship a placeholder domain.

## Editorial review of this draft

- Sample A: four cards, limited to visual style and an explicit exploration goal.
- Sample B: six cards, adding purchase and fit detail without unsupported sale, price, or frequency claims.
- Sample C: five cards, showing that infrequent shopping can still support a substantial style analysis.
- All reports: actual outfits, inspiration, purchases, and stated preferences remain distinguishable. Brand identities are not guessed from photos.
- Voice: concrete descriptions lead; no generic praise, aesthetic catchphrases, or mandatory joke in each card.
- Remaining validation: these are editorial fixtures. They do not prove extraction quality, fit inference, recommendation improvement, export rendering, or recognition by real users.
